## Why

The linking dialogue authenticates two iroh endpoints and possession of a one-time secret, but nothing ties the authenticated inviting endpoint to the `PdnId` the payload names: a `PdnId` is 32 random bytes with no key material behind it, so a device handed a payload cannot tell the identity it believes it is joining from one an attacker names. This is the step both ADR-0011 and ADR-0012 mark as deferred — "the KERI proof of control over the identity" — and it is the last thing standing between the ceremony and an access boundary that survives a hostile inviter.

## What Changes

- **BREAKING** An identity is a KERI root autonomic identifier, and every device of it is a delegated identifier: creation incepts the root and the first device, and linking incepts the newcomer's device identifier as a delegate of the inviting device's identifier, anchored by a seal the inviter writes into its own key event log. The delegator of a device identifier is fixed at inception and never changes.
- **BREAKING** The linking protocol moves to `/pdn/linking/1`: the reply becomes a stream of frames — the key event replay from the root down to the inviting device, the anchoring event, the inviter's signature over a manifest of what it sent, and the bootstrap tickets — instead of one frame carrying tickets alone. The dial remains one call on one bidirectional stream with one exchange.
- **BREAKING** An identity's inception seals role-tagged digest commitments to exactly 2 device-replicated identity stores — the private-metadata directory and the data namespace — and `data-layer` compares each reply ticket's namespace against the corresponding commitment before importing either. A successful pair check returns one opaque consuming handle, so only the exact verified bytes and internally bound fallback address can be imported. The namespace identifier itself is a read capability and never enters the key event log or crosses the `data-layer` boundary. Connection metadata replicas created by pairing are not members of this set; they are discovered through the already verified directory. Without these commitments the chain proves who is answering and nothing about where the tickets lead.
- The dialing device verifies the whole chain before importing anything — every event's signatures under the key state its own log establishes, not only the structure of the chain — and records the accepted heads durably together with exact `KEL1` reconstruction sets and their delegation-seal index. A pinned head is security state: it remains independently verifiable before replica payloads arrive, outlives a failed attempt, and is removed only by an explicit act of forgetting an identity, never by rollback.
- Every confirmed device carries a tagged `DeviceBindingProof` over its exact `NodeId`. A linked proof has 3 signatures: the canonical newcomer request, the inviter manifest, and a newcomer confirmation made only after the reply, store commitments, and tickets verify. Pending carries only the first 2 and is not a confirmable credential by itself. The founding device, for which no network ceremony exists, signs a creation-domain endpoint statement and receives a root countersignature. The anchoring event alone cannot prove freshness or endpoint possession because a linking retry deliberately reuses it.
- Identifiers are self-addressing: an identity's root identifier and every device identifier is the profile-1 SAID derived from the size-correct dummy serialization of its inception, so the whole configuration — keys, thresholds, witness fields, and for a delegate its delegator — is bound into the identifier, and a retried linking converges on one identifier because it does not depend on where the anchor sits.
- **This change has a hard dependency on the active `durable-runtime-storage` change.** Documents and blobs are currently in memory and the endpoint is bound without a saved secret key, so nothing survives a process and the node id is new in every one. That prerequisite supplies persistent documents and blobs, a saved endpoint secret key, and a real reopen-on-the-same-directory restart test, and SHALL land before any implementation task here begins. The durable half is not optional: pinned heads, startup recovery, endpoint-bound proofs, and idempotent same-endpoint retry are security and correctness requirements.
- Key material becomes durable state: current and pre-rotated device keys, root keys, the assembled delegated inception, and the state of an in-flight linking attempt. Root keys are kept durable because rotating the root and recovering the first device — neither of them in this change — are impossible without them; an exported recovery copy is left to the change that first reads one.
- The runtime gains one explicit operation to forget an identity: the only path that removes its pinned heads, its ceremony context, and its key material, as against the rollback of a failed linking, which keeps all three on purpose.
- A retry against the same inviting device and authenticated endpoint reuses the same inception and produces 1 device identifier and 1 seal, however many times the network drops. The attempt record includes the local `NodeId`; if the runtime's endpoint identity differs before completion, that attempt and its device keys are abandoned before dialing and a fresh inception is assembled, while accepted identity heads remain pinned. The same device identifier presented from another endpoint is refused in pending and confirmed states; post-confirmation endpoint supersession remains a later change.
- The wire profile fixes a 65,536-byte encoded-message-body ceiling and one shared 61,440-byte signed-event-material ceiling covering exact event JSON plus its indexed signature in request, reply, stored `KEL1`, and durable reconstruction state; a 4-byte transport length prefix is outside the former. An accepted stored event is therefore always replayable through profile 1. The flat manifest inventory derives a 1,968-event bundle ceiling from the body limit, including its 33-byte growth per event. The bundle byte count includes every encoded reply-frame body and excludes length prefixes and the request. Further policy choices bound bytes per bundle, delegation depth, and events per identifier. Applicable bounds, including the prospective post-anchor manifest size, are checked when an invite is minted, under the inviter's log lock before anchoring, by both protocol readers, and by storage/accepted-view admission.
- `link` gains one more typed outcome: the verification material did not line up. A chain that does not reach the payload's identity, an anchor over a different digest, and an exceeded limit are different facts and are reported as such; bootstrap-ticket failures instead preserve every failed role in deterministic directory-then-data order, while the inviter's refusals stay uniform.
- Key events are stored in the identity's directory replica under content-addressed keys, so two versions of one event coexist as separate entries instead of collapsing; the accepted branch lives in a durable local view, and a competing version is retained and surfaced.
- Full device access is no longer granted by the mere existence of `devices/<node-id>`. Record-level device listing stays payload-independent, but `SessionAccess::Full` requires the authenticated `NodeId`, a confirmed record carrying the complete applicable proof — 3 signatures for `Linked`, 2 for `Created` — and an accepted anchored device chain to the hosted identity. `pdn-node` publishes that joined result through a whole-identity, generation-bound `IdentityAccessProjection`; `data-layer` hosts closed by default, installs Full only after durable KERI state, and closes before any authorization loss, invalidating deposited writes and active Full sessions across crashes. Copying a pending offer cannot synthesize newcomer confirmation, copying a legitimate payload under another node-id path fails, and several valid confirmed endpoints for 1 device identifier fail closed rather than electing a winner.
- Startup recovery completes every unfinished creation record before the service accepts calls, but it does not make the next `create()` a retry. The existing call has no idempotency key: recovered identities are discovered through `hosted_identities`, and every newly admitted call creates another identity.
- The specs state what the ceremony establishes and what it leaves open — a substituted payload, a photographed secret, a forked history nobody showed us, a leaked directory ticket — so that the word "KERI" is not read as covering them.
- Deliberately out of scope, and named so the specs do not imply otherwise: witnesses, receipts, witness-accountability thresholds, and multi-key signing thresholds; duplicity detection across controllers; resolving or verifying a counterparty's device identifiers outside the hosted identity's own access classification; key rotation; revocation and device removal; `did:keri` in UWill; any change to the form of `PdnId`; caller-idempotent `create`; and any operation that resolves, migrates, or supersedes a confirmed endpoint. A linked device can later be forgotten and linked as a new device from a live inviter, but this change gives a founder no safe self-recovery after loss of its confirmed endpoint key: forgetting it also removes the root keys, and re-inception under the root is the deferred recovery operation. This change does replicate the immutable ceremony binding required by its own access boundary. The profile fixes signing threshold 1 and witness tally 0. Pairing keeps its own deferred proof.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities
- `pdn-node-keri-identity`: the identity model on the runtime — the root identifier and its delegated device identifiers, commitments to its 2 device-replicated stores, self-addressing identifiers with a pinned derivation, both device-binding proof variants, durable key storage, the four transactional `keri-v1` runtime-security record families, startup recovery of creation records, idempotent same-endpoint linking attempts, the explicit forget operation, and the local validated view whose pinned heads decide what a later replay is allowed to say.
- `pdn-node-keri-wire-profile`: the normative KERI 1.0 JSON bytes, algorithms and CESR codes, canonical `KEL1`/`PDL1` containers and their shared event bound, application transcripts, exact store-commitment construction, pinned `keripy` compatibility oracle, and checked golden vector.
- `data-layer-kel-store`: how key events live in an identity's directory replica — the explicit `kel/` entry family, canonical `KEL1` payload container, content-addressed entry keys, retention of competing versions, and raw read inputs consumed by the runtime-owned validated view.
- `data-layer-identity-store-bootstrap`: the durable prepare/bind seam for the 2 stores created before a `PdnId` exists, commitment computation below the capability boundary, and a byte-bound opaque verified-ticket pair consumed by its recoverable import.
- `pdn-layer-claim-model`: removal of the empty `PdnIdentityProof` placeholder, the exact retained `Claim` shape, rejection of the old serialized shape, and the rule that `issued_by` is attribution rather than verified KERI authority.

### Modified Capabilities
- `pdn-node-device-linking`: the ceremony becomes the `/pdn/linking/1` exchange described above — delegated inception and nonce in the request, anchor before the reply, streamed proof, three transcripts, the bounds at three points, verification before import (signatures, chain, and the tickets against the sealed store set), pinned heads that survive rollback, and durable boundaries for both `create` and `link`. The requirement heading that calls the invite "bearer-free" is corrected: the payload carries no long-lived capability, while the one-time secret is itself a short-lived bearer credential.
- `pdn-node-core`: the identity service creates an identity by inception rather than by minting a random identifier, offers the explicit forget operation, and its `link` reports the new verification outcome.
- `data-layer-private-metadata-store`: the directory gains key event entries, pending offers, and confirmed records carrying complete signed `DeviceBindingProof`; a device identifier cannot move to another endpoint in this change, conflicting confirmed bindings fail closed, and confirmation's first write remains the linking commit point; device listing stays record-level while full session access verifies newcomer confirmation and accepted KERI authority.
- `pdn-node-http-host`: the closed error table maps the verification outcome to a refusal rather than to an unrecognized failure, and the debug surface covers the forget operation — which requires saying, in the requirement that forbids a state reset, why calling the runtime's own operation is not one.

## Impact

- `crates/pdn-node`: `identity.rs` (creation by inception, keystore, attempt state, accepted heads and durable KEL problems), `linking.rs` (the v1 protocol, streamed reply, transcripts, limits, verification, store-set check, atomic ceremony records, verified pending observations and supersession), `runtime.rs` (durable state at spawn and shutdown), and `pairing.rs` only where linking reuses pending-invite machinery — pairing itself is unchanged.
- `crates/data-layer`: `private_metadata.rs` (canonical `KEL1` key-event entries, both tagged device-record proof payloads, 1 pending record per device identifier, the order of the 2 confirmation writes), `node.rs` (prepare replicas before a `PdnId` exists, bind a prepared data replica afterwards, compute opaque role-tagged store commitments, return and consume a byte-bound `VerifiedBootstrapPair`, and import without surfacing namespace identifiers or decoded tickets above `data-layer`), and the raw read paths consumed by the validated view. The existing grant-import paths remain unchanged.
- `crates/data-layer/src/access.rs`: record-level device listing remains available before payloads arrive, but the confirmed-record-only path to `SessionAccess::Full` is replaced by an opaque whole-identity projection supplied by `pdn-node`, with monotone generations, closed-first authorization loss, deposited-admission invalidation, and active-session termination; `node.rs::host_identity` installs a closed initial projection rather than deriving authority from records.
- `crates/pdn-types`: `Aid` gains meaning and a qualified form at the KERI boundary, and its doc comment stops describing it as an inception public key; the empty `PdnIdentityProof` type is removed; `PdnId` keeps its 32-byte form, with a canonical mapping in both directions.
- `crates/pdn-node-http`: one new mapping in the error table, and one new route for the forget operation, since the debug surface is required to cover the runtime's operations.
- `crates/pdn-layer`: `Claim::proof_of_issued_by` is removed, the former serialized shape is rejected, and `issued_by` remains non-authorizing attribution; existing `PdnOp::{AddDevice, RevokeDevice, RotateKey, ActiveDevices}` remain reserved and disconnected from the KERI identity service; they are neither emitted nor accepted as aliases for create/link/forget until a later versioned domain-API change defines them. `Connection.peer_devices` remains outside this change.
- New dependency: a narrow implementation of the fixed 3-event profile over the workspace's Apache/MIT-compatible BLAKE3 and Ed25519 primitives. `keriox` is not linked because its EUPL-1.2 licensing is unnecessary for this subset. The wire result is fixed by `pdn-node-keri-wire-profile` and cross-checked against pinned `keripy` as an external oracle.
- ADR: ADR-0003 now records the accepted KERI autonomic-namespace and device-delegation shape. ADR-0012 remains the historical version-0 ceremony decision until this change lands.
