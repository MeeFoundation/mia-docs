# Test arrangement: reach state through the product path

An arrange or act step reaches a namespace the way the product reaches it. When a scenario needs a granted namespace on some node, the grant surface and the grant binder are what put it there — publish the grant, let the binder import what it names, and poll for convergence. A hand-made handover (`share` a ticket on one runtime, `import` it on another) is a shortcut to a state the product would have produced, and a test arranged that way keeps passing after the mechanism it stands in for breaks: the binder can stop importing entirely and the assertions still see the data the ticket delivered.

A hand-made handover is admissible in exactly two places. First, where the out-of-band path is itself the subject — the data service's share/import handover has its own scenarios, and they rest on a bare ticket on purpose. Second, where it is the negative control the [access-control-tests](access-control-tests.md) practice requires — the outsider holding a ticket and no grant, whose ticket must address the very device under test and has no grant to carry it. Anywhere else, arrange through the product path.

An admitted instrument is named, never silent: the test's docs say what the hand-made step is for and why the product path cannot supply it (`pdn-node/tests/linking.rs`, the outsider control of the sibling-serving variant, is the worked example — the control needs addressing to the serving device, and no grant exists toward the outsider to carry it). Observation-only helpers behind the `test-util` feature (forced writes past the courtesy check, contact-set reads) are instruments of the same kind: admitted, annotated, absent from product builds.

Every rewrite from a hand-made arrangement onto the product path is checked against the deliberately broken mechanism before it is accepted — the binder disabled, the sweep reverted — and must fail there. That check is the only thing that distinguishes a rewrite from a rearrangement: if the assertions still pass with the mechanism removed, they were never testing it.

The worked example of the whole rule is `pdn-node/tests/sibling_serving.rs`: connection, grant, and granted claim all cross by establishment, the pair, and device replication; no import act appears anywhere; the serving device is isolated by taking its sibling offline rather than by aiming a ticket at it; and the outsider control beside it is the one admitted ticket.
